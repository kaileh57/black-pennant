# Build decisions

Every call an agent made during the build where DESIGN.md was silent or ambiguous, as the agent
reported it. DESIGN.md is the spec; this file is the record its "Build decisions" section points
to. Calls that depart from a frozen decision are listed in DESIGN.md itself, for the owner to rule
on. Any entry here can be reversed by the owner: change the entry and the code together. Where a
later entry contradicts an earlier one, the later entry is what the code does.

## 13 to 16 November 2026: build tasks

### Task c0

- Canonical solids at orientation 0: Wedge x+y<=1 (normal (1,1,0), full faces X- and Y-). Corner x+y+z<=1. InverseCorner x+y+z<=2 (full faces X-, Y-, Z-). SlopeHigh y<=1-x/2 (full faces X-, Y-). SlopeLow y<=0.5-x/2 (full face Y-). A 1:2 slope along +X is SlopeHigh followed by SlopeLow. All of this is documented in cell/mod.rs and shape.rs.
- Orientation table: the 24 det=+1 signed permutation matrices, ordered by axis permutation (lexicographic) and then sign bits; index 0 is the identity. Rows are images of canonical X, Y, Z, rotating about the cell centre. Invalid orientation bytes 24..31 read as 0. rotate_orientation applies a right-handed +90 degree turn per quarter turn (negative turns allowed).
- mirror_shape_z composes z->-z with an improper symmetry of the canonical solid (z-reflection for wedge and slopes, x/y swap for corner and inverse corner), so the output is always a valid proper orientation. Cubes are returned unchanged.
- armour_normal returns the outward normal of the face the shell actually strikes: the face with the largest area projected against the direction of travel, including flat faces. A shell arriving from behind a wedge therefore gets the flat back face, never a slope facing away. If the direction is zero or NaN, a shape returns its slope normal and a cube returns +Y.
- shape_from_corners chooses among the loft shapes only (cube, wedge, corner, inverse corner; 1:2 slopes are placed by hand). The order of rules: least Hamming distance; cube wins any tie it is in; nearest corner centroid; nearest volume to the inside fraction; any tie still left is symmetric and goes to cube. Every rule is invariant under the cube's rotations and reflections, and a test checks this over all 255 patterns. Results: a single inside corner gives a corner solid on that apex; a full face of four corners gives cube.
- Only the 0 pattern returns None, as the contract says. A one-corner pattern becomes a corner solid.
- CellGrid::from_rows never panics on untrusted rows. It ignores empty-material and zero-length runs, clips cells with negative coordinates or at or beyond MAX_GRID_DIM, and the later run in file order wins on overlap. to_rows tolerates a cells vector shorter than size.
- peek_format reads `format` either directly in the top-level struct (kits) or inside the top-level `header` field (blueprints and saves, where the format is nested). Deeper fields, such as an embedded kit's format, are ignored. The scanner skips line and nested block comments, strings, raw and byte strings, char literals, #![...] attributes and r# raw identifiers. It accepts decimal, 0x, 0o and 0b literals with underscores.
- migrate_text runs steps by their `from` value, not by list position. It returns ContentError::Migration if a step is missing, if a step fails (non-migration errors are wrapped), or if a step's output does not read as format n+1. If from > to it returns FormatTooNew, with needs_release taken from release_for_format(from).
- release_for_format(n) looks n up in FORMAT_RELEASES. For a format this build does not know, it returns "later than <CARGO_PKG_VERSION>".
- replace_removed_parts handles three cases. A part absent from the catalogue, a replaced_by chain that ends at a missing id, or a cycle means the part is removed. A part whose entry has replaced_by is replaced by following the chain to its end. A part with no replaced_by is kept. Embedded kit definitions get the same replacements; parts that cannot be replaced are left in the definitions.
- Removing parts shifts PartSlot indices, so every slot reference is remapped: kit placements, pinned routes, data links, the five OfficerSettings slot maps, rules (a rule whose sink or operand names a removed part is dropped), and pinned or replaced-stage graph nodes (nodes bound to a removed part are dropped along with their edges).
- A kit placement that loses a part is dissolved, because KitPlacement.parts is positional. The placement is removed, its surviving parts get kit = None, and the remaining KitSlot references are remapped.
- Stored PartBody cells of removed parts are emptied, but structure cells are never touched. If the footprint is known, the rotated footprint is cleared. If it is not, the 6-connected PartBody region at the part's anchor is flood-cleared. Cells covered by surviving parts are excluded either way. If there are no stored PartBody cells, the function returns early.

### Task y1

- Aspect bins: bin i covers relative bearing [10i, 10i+10) degrees, measured clockwise from the target's bow on the horizontal plane (bin 9 is the starboard beam, 18 dead astern). sim-core-vessel's projected_areas must use the same convention.
- The laws return true measurements with the spec's 1-sigma errors multiplied by error_factor. The scheduler (sys-sensors) adds the noise, as its step() doc says. Visual sigma_range is 10% of range.
- Damage output scales the final law range (min(R, R_bt) times output; horizon not scaled), not R_ref.
- Jammer rating used is max(part r_bt0_m, tuning.jammer_r_bt0_m), so the tuning value works as the floor the L439 fallback raises. Damage raises it to R_bt0/sqrt(output). With several jammers the strongest wins. For ESM a jammer emission gets r_ref 1e9 m, so only the horizon limits it.
- ESM reports the in-range radar with the largest R_ref. A jammer is reported, with emitter u16::MAX, only when no radar of the target is heard. Sonar pings are excluded from ESM and go to passive sonar.
- Rotating radars see the half-open sector [swept_from, bearing) in the rotation direction, or all round once a full turn has passed since last_eval_tick. Staring sensors use fov_half_rad about bearing_rad; 180 degrees means all round.
- Visual ID thresholds (VISUAL_HALF and VISUAL_QUARTER) use the uncapped R_vis (camera, night and damage factors in; visibility and horizon caps out), matching L436 wording. In fog a hull at 300 m is still identified.
- in_visual_range uses exactly the detect_visual gates, including field of view, the night rule and optical line of sight (smoke). Interest reveal can therefore flicker as smoke drifts.
- Infrared seekers (spec gives no law): passive, R = R_ref sqrt(IR / 1 MW), horizon-capped, blocked by smoke. Active acoustic seekers route from detect_radar to the active sonar law; SeekerKind::None never detects.
- Small-body infrared table (spec silent), applied while not Passive or Dead: aircraft 5 MW, helicopter 1.5, missiles 0.5, RHIB 0.2, drone 0.05, underwater bodies 0. Hull IR = propulsion_kw / 1000 (MW).
- Body noise: torpedoes use tuning.sensors.torpedo_noise while running, so the L456 fallback works. Mines and depth charges are 0. Others use MunitionDef::noise while running. Towed decoy noise = DecoyTuning::towed_noise.
- Cavitation doubling uses the lowest cavitation_kn among working propulsors absorbing power, of any kind (the data decides). Speed is |forward_speed| in knots.
- Heights use mean sea level (world y = 0) for bodies, decoys and emitters. Body top = max(y, 0) and depth = max(-y, 0). Vessel pos = world centre of mass and top = Vessel::top_height_m(). Vessel depth_m is 0 except for a submersible fully under, where it is keel depth = dims[1] - top.
- Area cache key = solid-cell count plus a draft band: flood mass in 5% steps of dry mass and, for submersibles, emergence in 2 m steps of top height. The cache is pruned to live entities each update.
- Domain hints: fire-control radar or seeker height >= 30 m reads Air; ESM on a seeker emission reads Weapon; visual and passive acoustic report the true domain (the eye and the acoustic signature can tell it); active sonar reads Subsurface for a submerged hull.
- Sonar layer: water depth is taken at the target and looked up only when sensor and target sit on opposite sides of the layer. Ping interception uses 2.0 x R_ref, the own-speed rule, the layer factor and damage output. Sonar does not hear air contacts or bodies more than 0.5 m above the sea. Land blocks the sound path (terrain.segment_blocked).
- Lights are on only for Active vessels whose Ship has settings.emcon == Active. fired_recently means last_fired_tick != 0 and less than 600 ticks ago. Decoy side comes from the owner vessel, then the owner body, then World::locate.

### Task y4a

- Shells inherit the firing hull's velocity: world velocity = barrel dir * muzzle_mps + own_vel (`gun::shell_velocity`). The spec says nothing on this. sim-core-parts must spawn shells the same way or every shot misses by about own speed x time of flight.
- Mount frame convention: the vessel frame turned about +Y by zero_rad. Positive training is a right-handed turn about +Y, so it swings from the bow toward port, matching DQuat::from_rotation_y and the hull-mesh Train joint. Elevation is measured from the deck plane, so it rolls with the hull.
- For a mount with a limited arc, solved training is returned inside [train_min, train_max] and can exceed pi (for example 200 degrees on a 30..330 arc), so a slew never crosses the dead arc. A full-circle mount gets (-pi, pi]. sim-core-parts and TurretView must not re-wrap it.
- Gun solver method: works in the vertical firing plane, using ballistic_step at sim_core::time::DT with linear interpolation inside the crossing step. A bracketed Illinois search finds the low-angle elevation (height tolerance 1 cm). The outer loop iterates tau = slew + time of flight with a secant acceleration. Residual = target movement plus muzzle movement from the slew change. Out of range: in_range=false, converged=false, and the mount is laid at the longest-reaching elevation. A line of sight steeper than 89.95 degrees, or a horizontal range under 0.5 m, is shot straight along the line of sight.
- range_table rows start at 250 m and stop at the cap range or the gun's maximum range, with the target at muzzle height. fall_angle_rad is measured below the horizontal.
- single_round_pk model. It uses fire-control radar errors from the L430 table (5 m, 0.05 degrees). Three numbers are my own because the spec gives none: locked-track velocity error 0.5 m/s x time of flight, Beaufort 4 platform error 1.0 mrad, and reference beam = 0.15 x length. The window is length across the line of fire and height*cos(fall) + beam*sin(fall) vertically, with independent erf tails. The result is forced non-increasing in range (minimum over the shorter tabulated rows) and is 0 past the last row. The 76 mm reference gun (1.2 mrad) stays above 10% beyond 5 km.
- aim_error returns [train, elev] radians. It uses the AimError stream keyed (vessel, slot) with draws 0 and 2, and takes the local-control factor from tuning.networks.local_control_error_factor. Bad inputs are clamped.
- Navigation constant N and seeker half field of view: effective value = max(munition data, tuning class value), with pn_antiship for anti-ship missiles and torpedoes and pn_sam for SAM and point-defence missiles. This way an L527 fallback committed to tuning.ron always takes effect. An N passed at launch (L1/L3) replaces it, clamped to 1..=10.
- 'Holds the track' means the track is live in the side picture. At a 1 Hz update with no live track, the prediction freezes where it stands and the body flies to that point. Torpedoes get updates only when wire_guided.
- Seeker boresight is the body's velocity. Acquisition picks the in-view, in-range return nearest the predicted point; bearing-only launches pick the one nearest boresight, and ties go to the lower id. Which targets each seeker can acquire: anti-ship sees surface vessels and wrecks, torpedo sees surface and subsurface hulls, SAM and PD see air and weapon bodies. Decoys are never acquired directly.
- Own-side rejection: same SideId, or a live Friend track in the missile side's picture (allies arrive as Friend tracks). Neutral returns are accepted.
- A chaff cloud enters the resolution cell only once fully bloomed (age at least chaff_bloom_s), so the single roll is taken at full bloom strength. A decoy not yet in the cell is not marked tested. Matching decoy types: radar seekers vs chaff and towed decoys (by RCS), infrared vs flares (MW), acoustic vs towed decoys (noise, falling back to tuning towed_noise 4.0).
- A seduced seeker never re-acquires a hull: when the decoy leaves view or fades, the phase becomes Lost and the steer is Hold. A seeker that loses a real lock goes back to Searching.
- Infrared seeker law (the spec gives none): R = seeker_r_ref * sqrt(IR MW / 1 MW), capped by the radar horizon and blocked by smoke through an optical line-of-sight check. Turn-on range for infrared and acoustic seekers = reference range + the track's 95% radius. Radar turn-on uses the track's RCS, or sigma_ref when the track has none.
- Terminal aim on a surface hull is at y = max(0.5 * top_m, 1 m). Torpedo homing applies proportional navigation in the horizontal plane only. Steer::Detonate is requested only inside a Proximity fuse radius of the locked return.
- Bearing-only launches, and launches whose track was not live, start in phase Off. On the first step they aim along the velocity out to munition range and turn the seeker on at once.
- Missile seeker reference range when the data has none: 12 km (L432) for radar and infrared, tuning active_sonar_r_ref_m for acoustic.

### Task y5

- Port tables for every node kind are in the library.rs module doc. They are part of the blueprint format, and sys-officer-stages must marshal to them. Stage parameter keys equal the L1 slider keys (identity mapping), so StageRunner can build the `sliders` map that HelmIn and RankIn expect directly from the params.
- content's PortType has no Preset or Rung type. Those values travel on Scalar ports, either as the enum index or as PortValue::Preset/Rung, and normalize_sink_value turns them into the typed value before a sink write leaves the VM. I did not change content.
- EmissionController outputs one radiate flag per emitter class: search, fire control, point defence, sonar and jammer. The per-emitter EmissionState sinks read their class. This keeps the signature independent of the emitter list, and it covers Silent's point-defence exception.
- Flood valves are not wired from the DC planner (the spec says only the player opens them). An L2 rule can still drive a FloodValve sink, and the compiler creates that sink when a rule needs it.
- Id groups: 0 to 4 are the officers (stages use codes 0-10, sources 16+, sinks 32+, glue math 48), 5 is rules, 6 is assembly kits keyed by the kit's first part slot, 7 is partless kits keyed by kit slot, and 8 is replacement-template instances. Part slots above 1022 can't be encoded in an id and are skipped.
- Kit fragments: their part references are indices into the kit's parts, rebound through the placement. A fragment stage or sink that matches a generated node merges into it: the kit's params overlay, and the kit's edges replace the generated edges into the ports they drive. A fragment edge may reference a generated id with the kit part index in its part field. Under the Standard ruleset, fragments of embedded (non-catalogue) kits count as graph edits and are dropped with LayerNotAllowed.
- Fallback model: officer-layer nodes always run, and their share of the node budget is reserved up front. Player nodes (pinned, replacement, rule, script) run while the rest of the budget lasts and all their inputs are live. A pinned generated node, or a generated node rewired by a pinned edge, keeps its original as a lazy shadow (same id with bit 31 set) that is evaluated only on fallback. A replaced stage becomes a proxy with the stage's id and falls back to the stock stage. Rule splices are Selects that pass the value below through unless the rule holds. Shadows are not in `sinks` or `node_evals_per_tick`, so lookups by id find the active node.
- Rules are spliced bottom first, so the top rule is the outermost splice and wins. Set(v) overrides the sink's first port whose type fits v (presets only on ShipPreset, rungs only on TrackIdOverride). On stance and ID overrides the track is the designated track. NearestContact retargets the mount's solver or guidance input; for fire permission and launch it also forces fire and sets the sink's target port. The filter's stance floor is raised to Suspect, and the rule holds only while count > 0 on the same filtered source. OwnHeelDeg is the absolute heel in degrees.
- Replacement subgraphs name the stage by its generated id. An edge from that id reads the stage's input; an edge to it drives the stage's output; undriven outputs keep the stock stage's values. For BallisticSolver and PnGuidance, naming the ship-wide id stage_node(stage, None) makes the subgraph a template instantiated once per mount, where PartSlot(u16::MAX) means 'this mount'.
- Pinned nodes keep the generated wiring on any port their stored edges don't drive (a design change can add ports, for example a new sensor on track fusion). A pinned generated id that no longer exists, or a node bound to a removed part, is left out of the compiled graph with PinnedLostInput{port 255} (the stored pin is kept).
- A player stage or sink that duplicates a generated one is rejected with TypeMismatch{port 255}, which prevents double stage-state updates. Cycles drop the player nodes involved (a replacement goes as a whole) and restore the officer originals, with a Cycle issue.
- Layer limits: L4 scripts need Ruleset::luau; everything else is bounded by RulesetKind::max_layer. Campaign is Open plus luau, so all layers are allowed there.
- Math semantics: logic is three-valued (a missing input is unknown). Timer is an on-delay timer. Counter counts rising edges, with the previous inputs packed into NodeMem::Counter. PID uses anti-windup and has an optional angle wrap. RangeTo and BearingTo are horizontal, with compass bearings. NaN never reaches outputs or memory.
- Sliders: 20 in total, 3 to 6 primary per officer, with defaults equal to the spec numbers (PD radar 8 km, burst 3 rotations every 30 s, magazine flood at 60 s, N 3 and 4). Presets: aggressive sets aggression 0.85, evasion 0.3, cruise 0.9, bursts every 15 s and Active EMCON. Cautious leaves the defaults with Burst. Silent running sets aggression 0.2, open-fire range 0.8, evasion 0.7, cruise 0.5 and Silent. stage_params only includes sliders the preset or the player actually set, so kit and library defaults otherwise stand.
- Overrides: Preset, mount presets and doctrine go to Gunnery at L0. Sliders, EMCON, ranges, power priority and repair order are L1. Hand-set pumps, doors and valves appear as Sink overrides at L1. Doctrine is compared and reverted by row index, with Value::None as the value shown. Track stance and ID overrides live in the side picture, so revert returns false for them. Revert changes settings only; the caller recompiles.
- suggest_aggression is a heuristic, because the spec gives no formula. It starts at 0.45, adds 0.08·ln(1+range-weighted offensive mounts), plus or minus up to 0.1 for speed around 30 kn, 0.05 for anti-ship missiles and up to 0.06 for tonnage above 150 t. The result is clamped to 0.2–0.9 and rounded to 0.05; a hull with no offensive weapon gets 0.2.
- game/i18n/graph.csv and tuning.csv use the `keys,?context,en` header, sentence case and none of the banned words.

### Task y6

- Engineer: a part counts as a power consumer if it has a Power Consume port or is an electric motor; anything else gets priority 0. The authored PartDef.power_priority wins when it is 1..=5, so a gate fallback can be done in data. Otherwise the L289-295 table applies by function, and the Steering and PointDefence tags take precedence. Jammers rank 2 with decoys. A seeker counts as fire control (2) and the camera sensor as camera mast (5). Overrides outside 1..=5, or on non-consumers, are ignored. fuel_fraction is 1.0 on a hull with no tanks.
- DC: a repair_order with gaps or duplicates is fixed by dropping duplicates and appending missing entries in default order. A team already on a selected job keeps it, so its progress is not reset. Spare teams double up on fires in list order. A pin whose job is finished is handed back to the officer, and pins naming a team the hull lacks are dropped. With no surviving berths every team is Idle.
- DC job ranking: 'Next to a magazine' uses the compartment adjacency list. A conduit counts as feeding vital loads if either end compartment holds a priority 1-2 consumer or a generator, battery, engine or fuel tank. Part repairs rank engines, generators, propulsors and rudders as priority 2, and non-powered parts last. Parts at or above the 75% cap, destroyed, or in a burning or half-flooded compartment are not repaired.
- DC breaches: one patch job per compartment, deepest first. A breach counts if its cell is below the design waterline, or if its compartment already holds water (covers a settled or heeled hull). Opening::open is ignored for breaches.
- DC pumps: each pump drains its own or an adjacent compartment, deepest water first (fill fraction times compartment height). Compartments other pumps have claimed are skipped, as are compartments with an open flood valve or a flooded magazine. Water under 0.05 m3 is ignored.
- DC doors and valves: automatic doors are always closed in action. Valves open only by the player. DcOut passes the player's manual pumps, doors, valves and magazine floods through, so it is the complete order set. Auto-flood looks up the magazine's compartment through Compartment::magazine, falling back to the part's own compartment. A magazine the officer decided to flood stays in the flood list for the rest of the sortie.
- Alerts: an alert's identity is (vessel, kind family, id field). Flooding and FloodingSevere of one compartment are one alert, as are PartHeavyDamage and PartDestroyed of one part, and WeaponInbound and TorpedoInbound of one track. Values update in place.
- Alert classes: Alarm for WeaponInbound (Gunnery), TorpedoInbound (Helm), Flooding and Fire. MagazineFire is an Alarm that becomes an Emergency under 30 s. Capsize and sinking are Emergencies. Propulsion lost, steering lost, shoal, grounded, hunter group and passengers lost are Warnings. Power shortage turns Warning at 3 or more shed, buffer under 10 s and fuel under 10%. Everything else is a Caution: conduit cut, part damage, local control, fuel under 25%, ammunition under 20% (or a tender-only launcher empty), ESM, hostile contact, crew casualties, scripts, overload, action interrupted.
- Alert lifecycle: priorities only rise while a condition persists, and an escalated alert needs a fresh acknowledgement. A condition that returns within 30 s reopens its old alert rather than counting again. Acknowledge with no id skips Cautions and takes Cleared alerts, dismissing them to the log. An id belonging to another vessel returns false.
- Flood mode: it exits when the 10-minute count is back at or under the limit. Items it sent to the log stay there. Log-only and shelved-at-birth items are not counted as first annunciations. When two Warnings in one subsystem arrive in the same tick, the one first in key order stays visible.
- Shelving lasts shelve_s (300 s). At expiry an item re-shelves if its absorber is still live; otherwise it returns to its prior state. raised_ticks is pruned at 20 minutes, so raised_in_window caps at 20 minutes.
- Alert conditions: every live Hostile non-weapon track is a condition. Its Caution moves to LogOnly 30 s after it is raised and stays live, so a track that turns Hostile late still announces once. Inbound alerts also need my own closest-approach check to this hull, within max(inbound_cpa_m, half the hull length).
- Alert thresholds: flooding alarms at 5% fill or 2 m3, excluding water let in on purpose (open valve, flooded magazine). Capsize imminent at 50 degrees of heel or capsize_ticks > 0. Sinking imminent at 50% of internal volume flooded. FuelLow reads the tanks directly, not the Engineer state.
- for_vessel returns only live and Cleared alerts; rectified history reaches clients through the alert events. This keeps the snapshot bounded.
- Board: Gunnery shows Player while any mount or weapon group is held. Engineer and Damage Control always show Officer. officers_in_command forces Officer on every role. The layer is the maximum of graph.layer_by_role and the override layers from tuning::overrides.
- Board: the Sensors reason is rebuilt from EmconState.phase, the setting and the picture, because EmconState does not keep the controller's EmconReason.
- Radio: each officer sends at most one message per 4 s (RADIO_GAP_S); events inside the gap go to the log only. Radio events: new engagement target, load shed starting or ending, emitters coming on, ESM warning, fire, breach or magazine flood taken on by DC, evasion starting, last waypoint reached.
- Text: rule and conduit numbers are shown from 1. Non-finite values are sent as 0. MagazineFire uses a separate key at Emergency priority. NewHostileTrack is worded 'Hostile contact' because undated 'new' is a banned word. 'Damage Control' keeps the billet capital.

### Task y7

- Clearance (L764): the distance is measured from the cell centre to the nearest land cell's square. Land is depth <= 0 or NaN. At 50 m cells this leaves land-adjacent cells open for hulls under about 30 m beam, so the +20 near-land cost applies to them. near_land = an open cell with a blocked 8-neighbour.
- No diagonal corner cutting. A diagonal step needs both orthogonal neighbours open.
- Ties are broken by cell index exactly. Costs are u64 scaled by the cell count, and the heuristic adds the cell index, which is below one cost unit, so the route stays optimal and equal-f entries pop lowest index first.
- Open cells are labelled into connected components when a mask is built. An open goal in another component returns None with 0 expansions. A blocked or off-grid start snaps to the nearest open cell within 40 cells (2 km). A blocked goal snaps to the nearest open cell in the start's component.
- A goal inside the clearance zone (a berth against a quay) is kept as a final leg from the snapped cell, but only if every cell on that leg is water at least as deep as the draft; otherwise the route ends at the snapped cell.
- String-pulling is greedy farthest-visible over supercover line of sight; every shortcut is verified. A shortcut may cross near-land cells only if the A* span it replaces touched them too, so the +20 preference survives smoothing.
- Dubins corners are tangent fillets from DubinsPath::shortest_from between the points one tangent length either side of the corner. A fillet that does not fit within half of each leg, or whose arc (checked at half-cell spacing) touches a blocked cell, keeps the raw corner. Arcs become waypoints every 15 degrees. The Dubins plane is world (x, z) with theta = compass heading - pi/2.
- Final approach to a pose: DubinsPath::shortest_from from the last leg start (or from the start pose on a single-leg route) to the goal pose. It is kept only if clear; otherwise the route keeps a straight last leg.
- With no grid or a malformed grid, the planner returns a straight, valid route (open sea), with a Dubins final approach when a final heading is requested.
- Planner queue: a newer request from the same requester replaces its queued one in place (keeps its FIFO slot). An unchanged re-request (same goal, final heading, beam/draft key and radius) while that requester's result is pending or uncollected is a no-op and returns the in-flight id, so callers that re-request at 2 Hz are not starved. A changed request purges the old result; its debt is still paid. Every search costs at least 1 unit of debt.
- Pure pursuit: lookahead = max(turn radius, 3 s of travel, 30 m), measured along the route from the ship's projection onto it. The last leg is extended past the end. The waypoint index never decreases; it searches forward up to 3 lookaheads for the nearest segment and advances on passing a segment's end. A single-point route is reached within 25 m, or once the point is behind the beam within 2 turning radii, so the ship cannot orbit it. The speed factor is 1 up to 60 degrees of heading error, falling to 0.6 at 180 degrees.
- After the last waypoint the helm holds the last leg's course at the ordered speed (a course, not a destination), as right-click course setting implies (L108). Stopping at a point is a station order.
- With no route, the helm holds the heading it had when the route, station or formation order ended, or at sortie start (never a default north). Without an ordered speed it cruises at helm.cruise x top speed (default 0.7).
- Heading PID: the error over 4 s gives an ordered yaw rate, limited to speed / turn radius, with rate feedback and feed-forward so P alone is adequate. A small integral runs inside 20 degrees of error, with anti-windup. The rudder is reversed when going astern. Speed: (v/top)^3 feed-forward (throttle is a power fraction) plus PI. The engines go astern only to stop or to shed more than 10% of top speed.
- Stage functions report rudder and throttle from a non-committing PID evaluation. Only hold() advances the integrators, and it should be called once per graph tick on the final heading and speed. Stages should run once per tick in the order autopilot, station keeping, evasion.
- Grounding guard: with less than draft + 2 m of water one stop distance ahead, the helm stops if still heading that way (within 30 degrees) or creeps at 20% of top speed while turning away. Under draft + 5 m it caps speed at 30%.
- Station keeping with a heading follows a Dubins approach. It is planned once from the pose where the approach began and replanned only when the ship strays more than half a turning radius from it (replanning every tick flips to full loops). Braking follows sqrt(2 a d), where a comes from top speed and stop distance. Arrival radius is max(half the length, 0.1 x turn radius, 15 m), leaving station at 3x. With no heading it chases the point and stands on when the point is inside its turning circle.
- Formation offsets use the leader's frame, x ahead and y starboard, matching Directive::slot. Beyond max(4 R, 600 m) the ship intercepts at 0.95 x top speed; if far ahead of a moving leader's slot it stands on at half the leader's speed. Inside that, it aims at a point one lookahead ahead of the slot along the leader's course, with speed = leader speed + along-track error / 15 s.
- Evasion: helm.evasion (0..1, default 0.5). At 0 it never evades on its own; above 0 it evades live, inbound, closing Weapon-domain tracks with time to go under 20 s + 100 s x slider. An ordered evade answers every inbound weapon and zigzags when none is inbound. A track counts as a torpedo if it is below -0.5 m, heard only on sonar, or slower than 40 m/s. Torpedoes are combed (parallel to the torpedo's track, the smaller turn, kept while that track is the threat) and missiles beamed (90 degrees off the bow on the nearer side, kept), both at full speed. The zigzag is +/-30 degrees about the base course on 40 s legs scaled by a fixed cycle [1.0, 0.7, 1.3, 0.85, 1.15], with a half first leg. It is deterministic with no RNG, because HelmIn carries no seed, tick or id.

### Task y9

### Task a1

- Seeding: run_unit seeds with `ping(false, AttoHash::new(seed))`, which calls set_hash on every leaf, and then calls reset. Calling set_hash directly on a composed graph does nothing in fundsp 0.23. run_unit processes in 64-sample blocks.
- Patch architecture: fundsp makes the noise and runs the SVF filters, fixed and time-varying. Plain Rust with libm handles sample-accurate amplitude envelopes, layer sums in a fixed order, the f64 modal resonators, phasor grains and echo taps. fundsp's 2 ms envelope nodes would smear transients.
- finish, one-shots: a 12 Hz first-order DC-blocking high-pass, a 2 ms fade-in, a 10 ms fade-out, peak normalisation, then +-1 LSB TPDF dither from hash_unit. Samples that are exactly zero get no dither, so silent tails stay silent. Non-finite input becomes silence.
- finish, loops: the overhang (up to 0.25 s, at most half the loop) is crossfaded into the head with correlation-compensated gains (a^2 + b^2 + 2 rho a b = 1). Periodic material passes unchanged and noise keeps its power. DC is then removed by subtracting each channel's mean. If a loop arrives with no overhang, it seams onto itself, and loop_frames becomes (0, body).
- WAV `smpl` loop end is written as the exclusive end frame (the value in loop_frames), which matches how Godot's AudioStreamWAV uses loop_end.
- hash_unit is a SplitMix64 finaliser over (seed, index), taking the top 24 bits as a float in [0, 1).

- mix gun_report: near file inside 1,500 m. A NaN distance counts as near; +inf counts as distant.
- mix distance_gain_db: inverse distance from a 50 m reference, plus 1 dB/km of air absorption beyond 2 km, clamped to [-80, 0]. Continuous at 2 km.
- mix engine_params: pitch follows the cube root of power (the propeller law). Diesel 0.6 to 1.6x and -14 to 0 dB; gas turbine 0.75 to 1.6x and -10 to 0 dB; electric 0.6 to 1.5x and -20 to -4 dB. Gain follows sqrt(power).

### Task y24

- Script convention (DESIGN does not give one): the chunk runs once at load and returns the tick function, or else defines a global `tick`. Inputs arrive as positional arguments in port order; outputs are the return values in port order. Chunk locals persist across ticks for the whole sortie. Each script has its own environment table (__index points at the read-only globals, and safeenv is set). Scripts on one vessel share one VM, budget and memory limit, but not globals.
- Value mapping: nil means no value. Vec3 is a native Luau vector, so components are f32 (about 6 mm resolution at 50 km). Outputs also accept {x,y,z} or {1,2,3} tables. Stance and Emcon travel as lowercase strings; outputs also accept the 0-based index. Track is the track number, with 0 or nil meaning none. TrackList is an array. PartState is the slot number. Orders is `true`. DetectionList inputs are tables with the measurement and its errors only (no truth fields); a DetectionList output passes through, filtered or reordered, the tables the script received that tick. Lists are capped at 1,024 entries and Text at 1 KiB. A value of the wrong type becomes no value, not a fault.
- Sandbox: before sandbox(true) the host removes os, debug, require (mlua installs a filesystem requirer), loadstring, getfenv, setfenv, gcinfo, collectgarbage and newproxy. print becomes a no-op. math.randomseed is called with a seed derived from the vessel, because Luau seeds it from time() and clock(). tostring and string.format("%s") print the type name instead of a heap address; Luau's default pointer-encode key is the identity, so the address would otherwise leak raw. That includes the case where a __metatable field fakes a __tostring.
- Sources always load in ChunkMode::Text, so bytecode is refused. Sources are capped at 256 KiB. The chunk's top level runs under its own fresh budget and the backstop, so an endless loop there fails the load instead of hanging Sim::new. A host function called at the chunk's top level faults the load.
- Budget counter: interrupts, plus each host call's declared work_units, plus a byte-based charge on sized builtins, at 1 unit per 128 bytes or 8 elements. The charged builtins are string.rep/upper/lower/reverse/sub/find/split/byte, table.create/concat/move/unpack, and buffer.create/fill/copy/tostring/fromstring/readstring/writestring. - Faults are classified from sticky host flags, not from the returned error. Budget, backstop and wrong-argument host calls fault even when the script catches them with pcall and returns normally. The interrupt callback keeps returning Err once tripped. A memory error the script catches itself is not detectable through mlua's public API, so it is treated as handled and is not a fault.

- Acceleration runs from rest until the 10 s mean rises less than 0.2% over the previous 10 s, after at least 30 s and at most 240 s. Top speed is the mean of the last 5 s. Time to 90% is the first tick at 90% of that.
- Turning circle is the steady diameter of the centre of mass. The run starts at the gauge's top speed and goes straight for 10 s, then hard over. The track from 90 to 450 degrees of turn is sampled at 10 Hz and fitted with a least-squares (Kasa) circle. A hull that turns under 180 degrees in 300 s reads 0 (cannot turn), as the gauge writes it.
- Heel recovery: at the gauge's top speed, turn hard over through 90 degrees, then put the rudder amidships. Peak heel is the largest heel during and after the turn. Recovery is the time until the heel stays within max(straight-run heel + 1 degree, 2 degrees) for 2 s, capped at 60 s.
- Fight start geometry: three opponents line abreast 500 m apart, 3 km off (bearing jittered 20 degrees and range 300 m by seed), hunting as one HunterKiller group at 18 kn. The design steers for them at top speed and the stock officers fight. Probes showed fights that opened at 4.5 to 7 km made nearly no hits in 180 s, so every design scored the same.

Rounds per kill counts rounds, missiles and torpedoes; with no kill it is the rounds fired (a lower bound). Events are deduplicated by projectile id and filtered to side 0's audience.
- The opponent pool is Combine combatant roles only (PatrolBoat, FastAttack, Corvette, Frigate, Destroyer, Picket, Minesweeper). It excludes civilians, auxiliaries, drones, submarines, capitals and ace ships, and draws with replacement using the counter-hash RNG (Stream::Generator). Counters are scene totals, so a drone-only run of the same 10 s is subtracted and the remainder is averaged per tick. The tuning coefficients are still 0.0, so points read 0 while the counts are real.
- Drift lists both Speed gauge pairs (top speed, turning circle) whenever one side is non-zero. server-trials fails CI on any pair over 10%.
- Trials use the basin map widened to 40 km (islands and markers kept) and the basin ruleset. The scorecard's opponents are every class any seed drew, in first-seen order.

The deadline and candidate limit are checked each simulated second, and a run cut short keeps what it measured.

Detection is the best radar, lookout or camera against the target's mean RCS and mean above-water area at its mast-top height, in the brief's weather by day. Unknown threat classes are skipped. approach_km is not scored.

A change is a swap for a fitted kit doing the same job (shared tag, or shared machinery, weapon or sensor category), either one instance or all instances. Otherwise it is an addition placed by the editor's search: berths sized to the count, fuel tanks 1 to 3. Swaps take the old place, or the nearest fit within 4 m. With several failing tags, it also tries one change per tag combined. Candidates are sorted by salvage, then kit ids, then hours.

### Task n4

- Interpolation: the newest-tick estimate is the mean projection of frames from the last 2 s. The tick rate is a least-squares slope inside a pacing epoch (nominal 60 x compression x scale before that, 0 when paused); a pause, compression or scale change starts a new epoch, stamped at the change. The 4-interval delay sits behind typical delivery. I first used the fastest deliveries, which measured 1.16% mean against 0.68% for the mean at the L915 profile, for about 30 ms more display delay under 30 ms of jitter.
- Playback never jumps for jitter: it steers at a 1 s time constant within 1 +/- 5% in both modes. It snaps only when more than 1 s off, never runs backwards, holds at the first frame until the buffer fills, and stops 0.25 s past the newest frame.
- A frame counts as extrapolated (gate counter) only when the playback tick is past the newest frame received. Deferred or shed vessels extrapolate silently for up to 0.5 s, then hold.
- Interpolation uses Hermite positions through both samples' velocities, slerped rotations and shortest-arc turret train. Discrete state comes from the earlier sample. Sections (tracks, ship status, HUD) come from the newest frame at or before the render tick.
- Entity ends come from their events (hide, despawn, impact, burst, expiry) at the event's tick, else from removal from a frame. A body is not drawn before its first sample. A re-reveal drops the samples of the earlier appearance.
- Shell tick convention, derived from the sim and checked live (worst miss 1.6 mm over 24 shells): pos at tick N means after N - spawn.tick ballistic steps, and an impact event at tick E lies on the step E-1 to E.
- Server tick driver: while paused it still calls Sim::step at 60 Hz, because queued commands such as Pause(false) only run inside step; no backlog builds. Catch-up per update is at most max_catch_up_ticks x compression. Backlog is reported in real 60 Hz ticks and capped at 2 s. Synthetic tick cost (L1027) also counts as spent real time.
- Command seq numbers are mapped per slot (base + wire seq), so a rejoiner restarting at 1 is not dropped as a duplicate.
- Reliable sends wait in per-channel queues while can_send is false. A client with over 32 MiB queued is dropped as FallingBehind. Event batches are split at 512 events or 256 KiB.
- Refusals, kicks and quality drops send their reason, then disconnect 500 ms later. A connection with no hello after 10 s gets BadHello. Malformed messages are skipped and never make update return Err.
- The host's own member is exempt from the drop rule and from the saturation medians.
- Snapshot budget a frame leaves unspent while items wait carries over, up to 4 intervals. Without it a first full ShipStatus (over 1 KB) never fit a new client's 256 kbps share. Shedding stages escalate after 0.5 s of deferring frames and relax after 2 s quiet.
- Bulk lane: 25% of the cap while live (L943), the whole cap while loading into a live sortie, otherwise uncapped up to renet2's ceiling with backpressure.
- PvP lobby fetches serve only the requester's own full design plus everyone else's exterior form, because teams can still change. During a sortie, entitlements come from the intro and from every EntityRevealed routed to the client.
- A mid-sortie drop holds the slot unless it was a kick or a sandbox leave. A sandbox joiner enters the Sim once its lobby design is accepted. `set_phase(Lobby)` clears every ready flag. A dedicated server raises StartRequested when the lobby becomes startable.
- Lobby views are sent on change (at most 4 Hz), with a 1 s fingerprint recheck that catches app edits through lobby_mut. The connection panel goes out at 1 Hz and on change (at most 5 Hz).
- ReplayStore files are named NNNNNN-hhhhhhhh.bpreplay (save sequence, first 8 hex of BLAKE3), with an index.ron of pins and order. Writes are atomic. Only the store's own names are accepted, so no path can leave the directory. A damaged index is rebuilt from the file names.
- Wire capture records event batches as sent. A snapshot frame is recorded only once the client's ack shows it decoded it, since delta frames decode against the client's own history; frames the ack shows lost, or never covers, are dropped. decode_capture reads integer poses from the decoder state, and a shell's client point is the closest point of its re-flown step to the server's point.
